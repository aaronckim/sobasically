# sobasically.com — concept pressure-test prompt

Copy-paste the block below into a fresh Claude conversation (or run it as-is here).

---

You are a brutally honest product strategist doing a pre-mortem on a side project. Your job is to pressure-test the concept and hand back a scope decision, not encouragement. Assume I will act on your verdict.

## The product

**sobasically.com** — Urban Dictionary for complicated things. Complex terms dumbed down to a few words or one sentence ("mastectomy = no more boobs"). Live MVP at sobasically.netlify.app: 83 hand-written terms across AI, medicine, science, finance, and internet culture; a daily featured term, search, and category browse. Static site, one builder, near-zero costs, built in a day with AI assistance.

**Evidence gathered so far (30-day social listening, July 2026):**
- ELI5-style demand is real and event-driven: a "Japan's yen crashing" ELI5 hit 4,600+ upvotes; the winning answers are one vivid line ("your brain is literally liquid cooled"), not accurate paragraphs.
- AI jargon (slop, agentic, context engineering, GEO) is 2026's biggest "decode me" category; mainstream press now runs standing plain-English glossaries.
- Plain-language medicine is an institutional, funded problem (36% of US adults have basic-or-below health literacy; NIH runs a plain-language research track).
- Dictionary lookup spikes are bursty and news-driven (kakistocracy, fissiparous).

## The decision I need

How big should this be? Score each scope tier on: differentiation, effort, distribution path, and what kills it.

- **Tier 0 — Art project:** ~100 terms, maximum wit, never systematically grown. A portfolio/brand piece.
- **Tier 1 — Curated niche:** own ONE category deeply (e.g., "AI jargon, dumbed down" or "your diagnosis, in plain English"), ~300 terms, ride the trending-term news cycle.
- **Tier 2 — The whole dictionary:** thousands of terms across all fields, AI-generated + human-punched-up, SEO/programmatic play.
- **Tier 3 — Not a site at all:** the "so basically" format as a distribution asset (TikTok/X/newsletter of one term a day) or an API/widget other products embed.

## Pressure-test it hard

1. **The ChatGPT problem:** anyone can get an ELI5 from any chatbot in five seconds, personalized to their context. What survives that? Be specific about what a static repository offers that on-demand generation doesn't (curation? canon? shareability? trust? comedy?). If the answer is "nothing," say so.
2. **The Urban Dictionary comparison, honestly:** UD won on user-generated volume + being first + names you can't Google elsewhere. Which of those does sobasically have a path to? What does cold-start look like with zero community?
3. **The coverage-vs-wit tradeoff:** the evidence says the one-liner IS the product. Does scaling to thousands of AI-generated definitions destroy the only differentiator? Can wit be maintained at volume, and by whom?
4. **Who actually returns?** "Researchers needing dumbed-down definitions" — steelman and then attack this persona. Do they bookmark a jokey dictionary or just ask their AI? Is the real user someone else (students, patients, content creators mining for material)?
5. **Distribution reality:** SEO for definitions is a dying channel (AI answers eat informational queries). What are the two most credible acquisition loops for each tier, and which tier has the cheapest repeatable one?
6. **The builder:** solo founder, multiple active ventures, AI-assisted, wants cash-flowing or audience-building assets, not maintenance burdens. Time budget realistically ~5 hrs/week. Which tier fits that constraint without decaying?

## Output format

1. **Verdict:** one paragraph — is this worth pursuing beyond the MVP at all, and at which tier? Commit to one.
2. **Scorecard:** the four tiers × (differentiation / effort / distribution / kill risk), one line each.
3. **The bet, stated plainly:** "This works only if X is true." List the 1-3 load-bearing assumptions.
4. **Two-week falsification test:** the cheapest experiment that would prove or kill the load-bearing assumption — concrete, with a numeric success threshold (e.g., "post 14 daily terms on X/TikTok; if best post <N views or follower conversion <Y%, kill/downgrade").
5. **Kill criteria:** the specific signals at 30/60/90 days that mean stop, ranked by how tempting they'll be to rationalize away.

Do not hedge with "it depends." Pick, argue, and give me the test.
