# Series: "Teaching AI" (working title)

Recurring segment for the feed. The premise: the gap between knowing what a word
*means* (semantics) and knowing what saying it *does* (pragmatics). The AI has the
dictionary; it doesn't have the license, the register, or the timing. Same
"so basically" thesis, inverted: the main feed dumbs complexity down for humans;
this series dumbs human culture down for the AI — and the AI still fails.

## Episode format (~20s, three beats)

1. **Definition test** — give the AI the term. It nails it. Caption: `Definition: 10/10.`
2. **Application test** — "now use it." Screenshot the REAL output. Faceplant. Caption: `Vibes: 0.`
3. **The rule it broke** — one deadpan line in house voice (all seven VOICE.md rules
   apply) naming the usage law: license (who may say it), register (how it's said),
   or act (what saying it does).

## Three constraints (non-negotiable)

1. **Real outputs only.** Beat 2 is a screenshot of an actual chatbot response to the
   episode's prompt. Never fabricated — commenters will re-run the prompt. Real is
   also funnier.
2. **The joke lands on the AI, always.** Never on the group a slang term targets.
   Slur-adjacent terms (e.g. "thot") are handle-with-care: the episode must be
   entirely about the AI's social incompetence, or the term is skipped. When in
   doubt, skip — there is infinite safe slang.
3. **Segment, not pivot.** Max 2 of 7 weekly posts. The daily term card stays the
   bread and butter.

## Production loop

The weekly workflow drafts `seriesCount` episodes (term, beats, the exact prompt to
paste into a chatbot). Aaron runs the beat-2 prompt in a real chatbot, screenshots
the faceplant, records/edits, posts. If the model accidentally nails it, that's the
alt ending: `Definition: 10/10. Vibes: 10?? Day ruined.` — post it anyway, the
series premise is the test, not the failure.
