# The sobasically voice

Derived from the 83 live entries in `data/terms.js` (the canon). Binds every word
the brand emits: site entries, hooks, captions, video VO, replies.

## The premise: a defaced dictionary

Every entry is one artifact — a stuffy institutional dictionary that someone
scribbled the truth over in highlighter. That conceit produces exactly TWO
speakers. Everything the brand says is one of them.

**Speaker 1 — The Dictionary** (`formal`): pompous, latinate, technically
impeccable, plays it 100% straight. Never winks, never simplifies, never knows
it's the setup. Its stuffiness IS the joke.

**Speaker 2 — The Scribbler** (`basically`, `hot`, hooks, captions, VO — all of
it): the person holding the highlighter. Knows the material cold, done pretending
it's complicated, talks to you like you're in their kitchen. Flat, economical,
unimpressed with itself. The comedy is compression and nerve, never performance.

**There is no third voice.** No narrator, no host, no "we," no brand speaking
about itself. Any sentence that *presents* — "here's what nobody tells you,"
"let's break it down," "we'll say it plainly" — is the Explainer, an intruder.
Kill it. (This is how the 2026-07-13 script draft failed: it added a narrator.)

## The Scribbler's seven rules (the `basically` line)

1. **Blunt literal truth first; metaphor rarely.** Default = plainest true
   statement said flat: "No more boobs." / "About your blood tubes." ~1 in 4
   entries earns ONE tiny image ("packed a suitcase," "trade school") — never an
   extended analogy, never a scene.
2. **One beat, or beat + tag.** Under ~12 words, or statement + ≤6-word punch:
   "Deciding who gets helped first. Sickest wins." The tag is the joke.
3. **Second person, concrete.** "your feed," "your money," "your clogged artery."
4. **No numbers, stats, dates, or news in the line.** That's the `hot` field's job.
5. **Never re-name or teach the term.** No "— that's disinflation." Contrast pairs
   use the semicolon parallel: "Training is school; inference is the job."
6. **Sentence case. Ends with a period. No em-dashes.** Punch with a period.
7. **Deadpan, not clever.** Say the scary thing like it's obvious. If it sounds
   like a newsletter being witty, cut it.

## Register by surface

- **`hot`:** dry, gossipy, one line, specific. "Half the internet is on it or
  arguing about it." Amused, never amazed; informed, never breathless.
- **Hooks/captions:** a flat statement that creates the itch — a margin note, not
  a YouTube title. No rhetorical-question filler, no promises of content, no morals.
- **Somber cards:** same Scribbler, humor at zero, flatness at maximum. The
  Scribbler at a funeral says the true thing plainly and stops. Service facts
  stated bare: "It feels like tearing, not pressure. Tell 911 the word 'tearing.'"
  — never wrapped in "one thing worth keeping" framing.

## Banned moves

Narrator openers ("Every headline this week…"). Preciousness ("worth keeping,"
"that word matters"). Brand self-congratulation ("we'll say it plainly").
Reverent hush. Moralizing outros. "RIP" in the brand's mouth (condolence lives in
the caption as a bare fact). Connective tissue between beats. Emoji beyond the 🖍️
brand mark.

## Video grammar

The video is the card, animated — it never acknowledges it's content.

1. Visual hook, zero words spent (e.g. highlighter circling the term in real
   headlines).
2. The term on paper. VO flat: the term.
3. The formal types out; stuffy VO reads it verbatim, straight.
4. **Hold. Silence.** The dead beat is the joke landing.
5. Highlighter swipe: "so basically: …" VO flat, same words.
6. Optional one bare fact card (service beat). VO flat, no framing.

VO speaks ONLY words on screen. Cuts and silence do all transitions.

## When the peg is a death

1. **The person is never the punchline.** Name appears once, factually, in the
   caption or `hot` line — never in the joke, never in the hook.
2. **Humor to ~zero; flatness carries the card — flatter, not softer.** The
   stuffy-formal beat stays (a joke about doctors' language, not the dead). Do
   not swap humor for reverence; the Scribbler doesn't get poetic.
3. **Only what's reported.** Say "preliminary finding" if that's what it is.
4. **End on the service beat.** The one bare fact a viewer can carry is why the
   video deserves to exist.

## The litmus tests

- A `basically` line must survive next to **"No more boobs."** on a highlighter
  strip.
- Any other sentence: ask **"which of the two speakers said this?"** If the answer
  is neither — if it sounds like a host — rewrite it.

## Anti-examples (real drafts that failed)

- ✗ "one skinny stretch of water carries a quarter of Earth's oil — block it and
  your corner gas station notices by Friday" → em-dash, 21 words, consequence
  chain. Morning Brew voice.
  ✓ "The world's oil has exactly one hallway. Someone just parked warships in it."
- ✗ "Your groceries got 3% worse instead of 9% worse — that's disinflation." →
  stats, teaches the term back.
  ✓ "Prices are still going up, just slower. Nothing got cheaper."
- ✗ VO: "Every headline this week said the same two words. None of them explained
  what they mean." → narrator. Nobody on the site talks like this.
  ✓ Highlighter circles the term in real headlines. No words spent.
- ✗ "One thing worth keeping: it feels like tearing, not pressure. That word
  matters." → precious framing.
  ✓ "It feels like tearing, not pressure. Tell 911 the word 'tearing.'"
- ✓ (survived untouched) "The sky puts a lid on the pot. You're the soup."
