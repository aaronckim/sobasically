# The sobasically voice

Derived from the 83 live entries in `data/terms.js` (the canon). These rules bind the
`basically` line. Hooks are feed captions — they're allowed to breathe. The `basically`
line is not.

## The seven rules

1. **Blunt literal truth first; metaphor rarely.** The default move is the plainest
   true statement, said flat: "No more boobs." / "Betting money that a stock will go
   down." / "About your blood tubes." Roughly 1 in 4 entries earns ONE tiny image
   ("packed a suitcase," "trade school," "Find-and-replace for DNA") — never an
   extended analogy, never a scene.
2. **One beat, or beat + tag.** Under ~12 words, or a statement followed by a punch
   tag of ≤6 words: "Deciding who gets helped first. Sickest wins." / "Prices go up
   AND the economy is stuck. Worst of both." The tag IS the joke.
3. **Second person, concrete.** "your feed," "your money," "your clogged artery,"
   "your crush." The reader is in the sentence.
4. **No numbers, stats, dates, or news in the line.** That's the `hot` field's job.
   The `basically` line is evergreen.
5. **Never re-name or teach the term.** No "— that's disinflation." No
   compare-and-contrast lecture. A contrast pair uses the semicolon parallel:
   "Training is school; inference is the job."
6. **Sentence case. Ends with a period. No em-dashes.** Punch with a period, not a
   dash. The em-dash cadence is explainer-newsletter voice — the thing this site
   is not.
7. **Deadpan, not clever.** The comedy is saying the scary or complex thing like
   it's obvious. If the line sounds like a newsletter being witty, cut it.

## The litmus test

Put the line next to **"No more boobs."** on a highlighter strip.
If it looks embarrassed to be there — too long, too eager, too clever — rewrite it.

## When the peg is a death

News-pegged medical terms will regularly involve a real person dying. Rules:

1. **The person is never the punchline.** Their name appears once, factually, in the
   caption or `hot` line — never in the joke, never in the hook.
2. **Humor budget drops to ~zero; bluntness carries the card.** The stuffy-formal
   register beat stays (that's a joke about doctors' language, not about the dead).
   The `basically` line goes blunt-grave, not witty.
3. **Only what's reported.** Say "preliminary finding" if that's what it is. No
   speculation about their health, habits, or last hours.
4. **End on the service beat.** The one warning sign or plain fact a viewer can
   carry ("sudden tearing chest or back pain — call 911") is the payoff and the
   taste. It's why the video deserves to exist.

## Anti-examples (real drafts that failed, 2026-07-13 run)

- ✗ "one skinny stretch of water carries a quarter of Earth's oil — block it and
  your corner gas station notices by Friday" → em-dash, 21 words, consequence
  chain. Morning Brew voice.
  ✓ "The world's oil has exactly one hallway. Someone just parked warships in it."
- ✗ "Prices are still going up, just slower. Your groceries got 3% worse instead of
  9% worse — that's disinflation. Deflation is when they're actually cheaper than
  last year." → three sentences, stats, teaches the term back.
  ✓ "Prices are still going up, just slower. Nothing got cheaper."
- ✓ (survived untouched) "The sky puts a lid on the pot. You're the soup."
