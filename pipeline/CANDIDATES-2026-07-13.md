## 2. disinflation (vs. deflation)  `finance`  (score 9)

**Why now:** The June CPI report (out July 14) may print a negative monthly headline because oil crashed ~21% after the ceasefire — and millions will misread one freak negative month as "prices are finally falling."

**Hook:** Tuesday's inflation number might come out NEGATIVE — and no, that does not mean stuff is getting cheaper.

**Formal:** Disinflation denotes a deceleration in the rate at which the general price level rises — inflation persists, merely at a diminished pace — whereas deflation denotes an outright decline in the general price level, i.e., a negative rate of inflation sustained over time.

**Basically (pick one):**
1. Prices are still going up, just slower. Your groceries got 3% worse instead of 9% worse — that's disinflation. Deflation is when they're actually cheaper than last year.
2. Disinflation: the car is still speeding, the driver just eased off the gas. Deflation: the car is rolling backwards.
3. Inflation on a diet vs. inflation in reverse.

**Best found in the wild:** “Disinflation means prices are still going up, just more slowly — your groceries got 3% more expensive instead of 9%. Deflation means prices actually fell below last year's. One negative month from an oil-price whiplash is neither.” — Kiplinger June CPI preview (paraphrase) (https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/economy/june-cpi-preview-dont-let-a-negative-headline-fool-you)

**Demand signal:** Kiplinger and IG both published preview explainers this week specifically to pre-empt the confusion
